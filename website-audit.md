# Top in Tech Website Audit — Conversion, Design & Trust Review

This is a full read-through of the current site (Vite + React + shadcn/Tailwind, pages: Home, About, Services, Team, Contact) with the goal you gave: turn small and medium business visitors into booked consultations or contact submissions, through easier navigation, clearer content, easier contact, and a more modern visual layer with animation and data visualization. The good news first — the design system underneath this site is already stronger than most agency templates. There's a full animation keyframe library (fade-in, float, glow-pulse, scroll marquee, scale-in), a scroll-triggered animated counter on the stats section, a working dark/light theme toggle, and English/German i18n already wired up. The gaps below are less about rebuilding and more about finishing what's already started, and fixing a few things that actively cost trust or leads.

## 1. Trust and credibility — the part that decides whether an SMB owner books

This is the area with the most conversion upside, and also the one flaw that isn't optional. There is no Impressum or Datenschutzerklärung (privacy policy) anywhere on the site. For a company legally based in Marburg, Germany, an Impressum is a legal requirement under German law (TMG/DDG), not a nice-to-have — its absence is the kind of thing a cautious SMB buyer (or a competitor) will notice immediately, and it's a compliance risk independent of marketing goals. This should be the first fix, ahead of any visual work.

Beyond that, the credibility signals an SMB buyer looks for before booking a call are largely missing. The stats section on the homepage claims "120+ AI Assistants Deployed," "45+ Voice Agents Live," and "3,000+ AI Images & Videos Generated," but the Team page shows a three-person team led by one founder, and there are no client names, logos, or links anywhere to back those numbers up. To a skeptical buyer doing five minutes of due diligence, unverifiable round numbers next to a very small team read as inflated rather than impressive, and they undercut the "no hype, just results" positioning the copy itself uses on the Services page. The fix isn't to remove all numbers — it's to either replace them with claims you can stand behind (years of combined experience, number of tools mastered, founding date) or to attach real case studies to them once you have three or four you can name.

The Services page already has a "Real Use Cases" block per service ("Marketing agency saves 20+ hours/week," "Medical clinic handles 100% missed calls"), which is a good instinct, but every single one is anonymous and unlinked. Even one full case study per service — company type, the problem, what you built, the measurable outcome, ideally with a named client or logo — would do more for conversion than any animation change. The "Tools we use" marquee (OpenAI, Claude, Gemini, n8n, etc.) is currently the closest thing to a logo wall on the page; it's honestly labeled as tools rather than clients, but a first-time visitor scanning quickly could easily misread it as a client roster. Once real client logos exist, they deserve their own clearly-labeled "Trusted by" section — don't let the two mix.

There's also no FAQ anywhere on the site. For AI/automation services sold to SMBs, the standard objections are predictable: is our data secure, do we need in-house technical staff, how long does implementation take, what does this cost, is there a contract commitment. Right now the only nod to this is a single "Secure & compliant systems / GDPR-ready" bullet on the About page. An FAQ section — which is also a perfect, low-effort use of the Accordion component that's already installed in `src/components/ui/accordion.tsx` but currently unused anywhere in the app — would directly reduce the hesitation that keeps a visitor from clicking "Book a Consultation."

Finally, there's no pricing information at all, not even a starting range. SMB buyers are price-sensitive and time-poor; many will simply leave rather than fill out a form to find out if they can afford you. You don't need a full pricing table — even "Projects typically start at €X" or three tier names without hard numbers pre-qualifies leads and measurably reduces bounce on service sites like this.

## 2. Contact and booking flow — two competing paths where there should be one

The site currently has two separate ways to "get started," and they behave inconsistently. The Contact page (`src/pages/Contact.tsx`) submits through a Supabase edge function (`submit-contact`) that forwards straight to an n8n webhook and, as things stand, never writes to the `contact_leads` table — so if the webhook has a bad moment, that lead is gone with no record on your side. Separately, the homepage's "Book Your Free AI Audit Call" section (`AuditSection.tsx`) has a real date-and-time picker but calls the n8n webhook directly from the browser, bypassing Supabase entirely, which means the webhook URL is visible to anyone who opens dev tools, and there's likewise no durable record if that call fails. On top of that, the time slots shown (09:00–16:30 in fixed 30-minute blocks) aren't checked against anything — there's no logic preventing two visitors from "booking" the same slot, which will eventually cause a real scheduling collision.

The most professional-feeling, lowest-effort fix here is to pick one booking mechanism and make it real. Embedding a proper scheduler (Cal.com or a similar calendar tool has a free embeddable widget) in place of the static time-slot buttons would immediately look more modern, remove the double-booking risk, and send automatic confirmations without you building that logic yourself. If you'd rather keep the custom-built picker, at minimum both the contact form and the booking form should write to Supabase first (the `audit_bookings` table from the schema I set up last session is ready for exactly this) and treat the n8n webhook as a notification, not the system of record.

It's also worth having only one primary call-to-action phrase used consistently. Right now the site alternates between "Get Free AI Audit," "Book a Free Consultation," "Talk to an AI Expert," and "Book My Free Audit Call" across the navbar, hero, CTA section, and Services page. That variety isn't wrong exactly, but consolidating around one primary offer (e.g., "Book Your Free AI Audit") with the others as secondary phrasing makes the ask clearer and easier to track in analytics.

Lastly, for a service business this size, a persistent way to reach you — a small floating WhatsApp or "Talk to us" button visible on every page, not just in the footer and Contact page — is now a standard pattern for "easy to contact," and it's a small addition given the WhatsApp link already exists in the Footer.

## 3. Navigation and structure

The top-level navigation itself is already in good shape — five items (Home, About, Services, Team, Contact) is the right amount for a site this size, and the sticky navbar with scroll-based blur/background is a nice modern touch. The main structural gap is the Services page: it's a single 329-line vertical stack of six full sections (AI Assistants, Voice Agents, Workflow Automation, Image & Video, Consultancy, Web & Mobile Apps), each with its own header, category grid, use cases, and benefit chips. A visitor who only cares about, say, voice agents has to scroll past five other services to get there, and there's no in-page way to jump around besides anchor links.

This is the clearest match for the "tabs" you asked about, and it's a very natural fit — `src/components/ui/tabs.tsx` (shadcn Tabs, Radix-based) is already installed and configured but isn't used anywhere in the codebase. Converting the six services into a tabbed interface (icon + label per tab, content panel below) would let a visitor see all six offerings at a glance and jump straight to the one relevant to their business, which is exactly the "easy to navigate, easy to understand" outcome you described. The existing anchor IDs (`#assistants`, `#voice`, etc., already linked from the Footer and homepage service cards) can still work by having the tab component sync to the URL hash on load.

The homepage itself has a clean, logical scroll order (Hero → Services → Stats → Tools → Booking → final CTA), so no restructuring needed there — just the trust and pricing content described above layered in.

## 4. Visual design, animation, and data visualization

The existing animation vocabulary is genuinely solid for a 2025-era SaaS/agency look: gradient text, glow borders, a grid-pattern hero background, floating icons, and a scroll-triggered animated counter (`StatsSection.tsx` uses an `IntersectionObserver` to count up numbers as they enter view, which is exactly the kind of "modern" touch you're asking for). The gap is that this treatment stops at the homepage stats section — the About and Services pages use only the initial page-load fade-ins, with no scroll-triggered reveals as the visitor scrolls further down long pages. Reusing the same `IntersectionObserver` pattern already built in `StatsSection.tsx` across the About "Why Us" grid, the Services category cards, and the Team grid would make the whole site feel consistently animated rather than only the homepage.

The bigger opportunity, and the one that directly answers "fancy charts," is that `recharts` is already a project dependency and `src/components/ui/chart.tsx` (a shadcn chart wrapper built on top of it) already exists in the codebase — and neither is used anywhere. This is effectively a feature you've already paid for in bundle size but aren't getting any benefit from. A real, animated chart would do more to signal "modern, data-driven company" than almost any other single change: a bar or line chart showing time saved / cost reduced per service on the Services page, a simple donut chart on the About or Home page illustrating "where SMBs typically lose hours before automation," or a small comparison chart (manual process vs. automated process, in hours or euros) next to the AI Voice Agents or Workflow Automation sections where you already have concrete numbers like "€2,000–€5,000/month" and "40% fewer no-shows." Recharts animates on mount by default, so this also reinforces the animated feel you're after without extra animation libraries.

Two smaller visual notes. First, both `fontFamily.sans` and `fontFamily.display` in `tailwind.config.ts` are set to the same system font stack (-apple-system, Segoe UI, Roboto, Arial). That's fine functionally, but it means headings and body text use an identical typeface, which slightly undercuts the "modern, designed" feel — pairing the existing body font with a distinct display/heading font (a geometric sans like Space Grotesk or Sora via a variable font import) is a low-risk, high-visual-impact change. Second, the Team page currently shows initials-in-a-circle instead of real photos for all three team members. For an SMB buyer trying to decide whether to trust a small agency, real (or at least professionally illustrated) headshots matter more than most other visual polish — this is worth prioritizing over decorative animation work.

## 5. Technical, SEO, and reliability details

A few smaller items worth fixing while in the code. `public/robots.txt` exists but has no `Sitemap:` directive, and there's no `sitemap.xml` in the project at all — for a local B2B service business that wants to rank for "AI automation Marburg" type searches, this is a quick, real SEO gain. None of the pages currently carry Organization/LocalBusiness structured data (JSON-LD), which is what lets Google show your business address, phone number, and rating directly in search results — worth adding given the site already has a concrete address and phone number in the Footer. There's also no analytics or conversion tracking (no Google Analytics, Meta Pixel, or similar) anywhere in the code, which means right now there's no way to actually measure whether any of these changes move the needle on bookings — this should probably be one of the first things added, before or alongside the redesign work, so you have a baseline.

Finally, the "Tools we use" marquee (`ToolsSlider.tsx`) hotlinks logo images directly from third-party CDNs (worldvectorlogo.com, npmmirror.com, Wikipedia, etc.) rather than hosting them yourself, with a silent `onError` fallback that just hides the image if the link breaks. That's a minor reliability risk (logos can silently disappear) and also means you're depending on external, unofficial hosting of other companies' brand marks — self-hosting these assets in `public/` would be more robust and lower-risk.

## Suggested priority order

If I were sequencing this as work, I'd go in three passes rather than one big rebuild.

**Pass 1 — trust and legal (highest impact, lowest design effort):** add an Impressum and privacy policy page, replace or substantiate the homepage stats, add a short FAQ using the already-installed Accordion component, and add at least a rough pricing signal. None of this requires new components or libraries — it's content and one new page.

**Pass 2 — conversion mechanics:** consolidate the two "book a call" paths into one (ideally a real embedded scheduler), make sure both contact and booking submissions persist to Supabase rather than depending solely on the n8n webhook, and add a persistent WhatsApp/contact affordance site-wide.

**Pass 3 — visual modernization:** rebuild the Services page around the existing Tabs component, add one or two recharts visualizations where you already have concrete numbers, extend the scroll-triggered reveal pattern from StatsSection across the other long pages, add a distinct display font, and replace the Team page initials with real photos.
